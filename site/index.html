    Why the estimate and the payout can differ

    Why a very small share is carried instead of sent

    A day’s share can come out smaller than the network fee to send it, and paying it would cost more than it is worth. Anything under is withheld, not forfeited: it stays credited to the address and goes out with a later day, as soon as the running total clears that threshold. Under daily settlement this is the ordinary case, not an unusual one.

    It is paid on the next day the address is eligible again — a callout, the minimum held, no lockout — so an address that stops qualifying leaves its carry sitting. Carry expires after 30 days and returns to the pool, and adding to it does not restart that clock: it runs from when the first amount was credited.

    Every carried amount is listed in that day’s carry.json, and each file records the hash of the one before it, so the ledger is append-only and can be checked end to end rather than a day at a time.

    the basics

    Three rules, that’s it

    Do all three on the same day and you are in that day’s split.

    1. 01

      Hold

      All day. The day counts at your lowest balance.

    2. 02

      Call it out on pump.fun

      Once a day, before 00:00 UTC. Ten calls count as one.

    3. 03

      Do not sell

      Go under the minimum and the next 7 days pay nothing.

    Dropping below the minimum locks you out for 7 days. Trimming is not a sale — stay at or above and you keep earning. Adding liquidity to this coin’s own pump.fun pool is the one exception, and does not lock you out.

    What counts as selling

    • Going under the minimum

      Selling or sending — only where your balance lands matters.

    • Tokens outside your main account

      Only your associated token account is counted. Nothing is summed.

    • Selling before the payout lands

      It re-checks your balance on arrival. One day of exposure.

    What happens in every case, today’s share and the 7-day lockout

    Two things are decided separately each day: what today is worth, and whether the next 7 days are locked. Only dropping below the minimum does both. Trimming your position while staying above it costs you today’s weight and nothing else.

    What you did today Today’s share Next 7 days
    Held your position, no changes Counted in full Keep earning
    Bought this coin for the first time today Scaled to the part of the day you held — you are in from today Keep earning
    Topped up an existing position Larger, counted from the moment the top-up landed Keep earning
    Supplied liquidity to this coin’s own pump.fun pool Counted — this is not a sale Keep earning
    Did not call out today Nothing — a call does not carry over Keep earning, as soon as you call out again
    Sold some, stayed at or above the minimum The whole day counts at your lowest balance Keep earning — this is not a sale
    Sold some, then topped back up the same day The hours before the sale still count at the lower balance; the top-up counts from the moment it lands Keep earning, if you never went under the minimum
    Dipped below the minimum, then bought back Counted, but the hours before the dip count at the low balance Locked out
    Sold everything Nothing Locked out
    Moved enough to another wallet to fall under the minimum Nothing — judged on where this wallet lands Locked out

    “Locked out” means the next 7 days earn nothing at all, whatever you hold and however often you call out. Buying back does not shorten it. It is triggered by one thing only: your balance ending up below the minimum. The lockout starts the day after that happens — on the day itself you are already losing the share, so the two penalties do not overlap.

    A worked example, with real numbers

    Eight wallets, each still holding at midnight, each having called out. The share column is what the day’s split is divided by — so a wallet with twice the number gets twice the money.

    Held 500,000, sold to 120,000 at 18:00 UTC, topped back up to 1,000,000 at 20:00 UTC 266,667 — the first 20 hours count at 120,000, the last 4 at 1,000,000. Never went under the minimum, so still earning tomorrow

    Bought 500,000 at 20:00 UTC 83,333 — four hours of the day
    Held 500,000, topped up to 5,000,000 one minute before midnight UTC 503,125 — a minute is worth a minute

    The rule behind all of it is one sentence: at any moment you are credited with the smallest balance you still hold for the rest of the day. That is why adding counts from when it lands and never before, and why selling reaches backwards across the whole day.

    The exact rule, and what each part of it stops 
    eligible(w, d)  =  called_out(w, d)
                   AND  sustained(w, d) >= 
                   AND  NOT locked(w, d)

sustained(w, d) =  the LOWEST balance w held while holding
                   anything during UTC day d
                   — this decides IF you are paid

hold(w, d)      =  the smallest balance w still holds for the
                   rest of the day, at each moment, averaged
                   across the whole day
                   — this decides HOW MUCH

locked(w, d)    =  w's balance ended BELOW the minimum at any
                   point during any of the 7 whole epochs
                   before d. Supplying liquidity to this coin's
                   own pump.fun pool is the one exception.

share(w, d)     =  divisible(d) × hold(w, d) / Σ hold(v, d)
                   rounded down, so the remainder stays in
                   the pool for tomorrow

divisible(d)    =  the pool's balance, less rent, less what
                   earlier days still owe, and less the carry
                   already promised
    The floor, as this page has it of the total supply, fixed on chain and immutable. It is a token count — there is no dollar figure and no price anywhere in this system.
    The floor, as the program enforces it
    Try this What happens
    Buy at 23:58 UTC, call out, sell at 00:02 UTC Buying at 23:58 UTC earns two minutes of the day, because your share is scaled by how long you held. Selling at 00:02 UTC takes you below the minimum, so the next 7 days earn nothing too.
    Call out once, then stop Calls do not carry over. Each day needs a callout or a callout update.
    Sell after being paid, then buy back If the sale took you below the minimum: 7 days of nothing, and buying back does not shorten it. If you stayed above it, you keep earning — that day is just counted at your lowest balance.
    Move tokens to a second wallet you own Judged on where the sending wallet lands, exactly like a sale. There is no netting across wallets.
    Split a bag across many wallets Changes nothing about what you earn — weight is linear. It does let you exit in increments, and that is a deliberate, known property, not an oversight.
    Push the price down at 23:59 to lower the floor The floor is a fixed token count, not a dollar value. There is no price oracle to move.

    the split

    Where the fees go

    Every trade pays a creator fee. 90% goes to the callers.

    90%

    To the callers

    Split every day among everyone who called it out and held their position through it.

    10%

    To running it

    Gas, a node, a host, a domain.

    Neither number can be changed — not even by us. pump.fun’s instruction sets the split and revokes its own authority in the same transaction.

    Why the 10% is not a founder’s fee, and what we still control

    It only pays anything if the coin trades. At $1M of weekly volume after graduation the whole creator fee is about $500 — roughly $450 to callers and $50 to infrastructure. If this coin does nothing, that line earns nothing and the job gets paid for out of pocket anyway, because the alternative is the settlement quietly stopping and nobody getting paid at all.

    In pump.fun’s own documentation, “this instruction can only effectively be used once.”

    What we do still control, and what we don’t. We post each day’s results, and the limits on that are under risks. We hold no authority over this split. pump.fun retains the same platform-level powers over this coin that they retain over every coin they host, including the ability to reassign a coin’s fee recipient — that is real, we cannot prevent it, and we would rather you read it here than discover it later.

    Check any of it yourself — four commands, no permission needed

    These are the commands that would catch us if any number on this page were wrong. They need no key and no permission from us.

    The pool balance, straight from a node

    solana balance <pool address> --url mainnet-beta

    The same account “Caller pool” above is read from, asked of a node of your choosing rather than ours. No part of this command talks to us, and the address it takes is printed under Addresses in the section above.

    Reproduce a day’s fingerprint from its published working

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --offline

    Reads only the files in snapshots/epoch-<n>/ and rebuilds the merkle root. If it does not match the root posted on chain, we published a number we cannot justify.

    Re-derive it from chain instead of trusting the directory

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --recheck-chain

    Replays every caller’s transfer history from a node and recomputes every hold. This is the check that does not take our word for anything except the callout list. It is the slow one, it is the one that matters, and it needs a node that will serve full signature history.

    Confirm your own callout exists, without us

    curl -H "x-api-key: <pump.fun's public key>" \
  https://api.coin-communities.xyz/api/v1/users/by-wallet/<address>/callouts

    The same request this page makes from your browser — pump.fun’s record of who called out, not ours, and the one input not derivable from chain history.

    risks

    What this is not

    The parts we would rather you read here than discover later.

    This is a memecoin. It can go to zero, and most do. What is described here is how creator fees are split — not a return, not a yield, not a projection of what the coin will be worth. Nothing here is financial advice.

    The caller list is attested, not trustless

    Every number here except one can be re-derived from public chain history. The exception is who called out — that exists only in pump.fun’s database, not on chain. Anyone can clone the GitHub repo and re-derive the same list the same way we do. We say attested rather than trustless because the input still comes from pump.fun’s API, not from immutable chain history — the second word would be false.

    Flags are pump.fun’s, and they are retroactive

    Callouts marked spam or harmful, or deleted, do not count. Those flags belong to pump.fun and can change after the fact. They are published in each day’s working so the rule stays checkable.

    If pump.fun removes callouts

    Everything earned up to that point stays claimable. Results already posted are unaffected — the program has no notion of callouts and cannot be switched off. New days would simply stop being created.
