    the basics

    How it works

    Three things. Do all three on a given day and you are in that day’s split; miss one and you are not.

    1. 1

      Call it out

      Post a callout for this coin on pump.fun. Each day counts on its own — one callout does not carry over to tomorrow.

    2. 2

      Hold

      All day, without dipping below it. What counts is your lowest balance between one midnight and the next, not what you hold at the end.

    3. 3

      Get paid

      Your share is worked out at 00:00 UTC and sent to you. You never claim anything and you never connect a wallet.

    Selling locks you out for 7 days. Any decrease counts, however small — including moving tokens to another wallet you own. Buying back does not shorten it.

    What counts as selling

    Any decrease, however small

    Sending tokens to another wallet you own counts. There is no netting, no summing across accounts, and no exemption for housekeeping. A decrease locks the wallet out for the next 7 days, and buying back does not shorten it.

    Only your main token account counts

    Tokens parked in any other token account earn nothing. The daily settlement and the on-chain check both read the wallet’s associated token account and nothing else.

    Sell out entirely and you cannot be paid

    The payout checks that you still hold the coin. Because rewards go out daily rather than monthly the exposure is one day — but it is real.

    The exact rule, and what each part of it stops 
    eligible(w, d)  =  called_out(w, d)
                   AND  hold(w, d) >= 
                   AND  NOT locked(w, d)

hold(w, d)      =  the MINIMUM balance of w's associated token
                   account at any point during UTC day d

locked(w, d)    =  w's balance decreased during any of the 7
                   whole epochs before d

share(w, d)     =  pool(d) × hold(w, d) / Σ hold(v, d)
    The floor, as this page has it of the total supply, fixed on chain and immutable. It is a token count — there is no dollar figure and no price anywhere in this system.

    Sell after being paid, then buy back 7 days of nothing. Buying back does not shorten it.
    Move tokens to a second wallet you own Counts as a sale on the sending wallet. There is no netting.
    Split a bag across many wallets Changes nothing about what you earn — weight is linear. It does let you exit in increments, and that is a deliberate, known property, not an oversight.

    the split

    Where the fees go

    Every trade on pump.fun pays a creator fee. Ours splits two ways, and the split was fixed before the coin ever traded.

    90%

    To the callers. Split every day among everyone who called it out and held their position through it.

    10%

    To running it. The gas that sends every reward out daily, a node that can take launch traffic, a host and a domain.

    Neither number can be changed — not even by us. pump.fun’s fee-sharing instruction sets the split and revokes its own authority in the same transaction. We wrote 90/10 once, and that was the only chance anyone had to write anything.

    Why the 10% is not a founder’s fee, and what we still control

    It only pays anything if the coin trades. At $1M of weekly volume after graduation the whole creator fee is about $500 — roughly $450 to callers and $50 to infrastructure. If this coin does nothing, that line earns nothing and the job gets paid for out of pocket anyway, because the alternative is the settlement quietly stopping and nobody getting paid at all.

    In pump.fun’s own documentation, “this instruction can only effectively be used once.”

    What we do still control, and what we don’t. We post each day’s results, and the limits on that are under risks. We hold no authority over this split. pump.fun retains the same platform-level powers over this coin that they retain over every coin they host, including the ability to reassign a coin’s fee recipient — that is real, we cannot prevent it, and we would rather you read it here than discover it later.

    Check any of it yourself — four commands, no permission needed

    These are the commands that would catch us if any number on this page were wrong. They need no key and no permission from us.

    The pool balance, straight from a node

    solana balance <pool address> --url devnet

    The same account the number above is read from. No part of this command talks to us.

    Reproduce a day’s fingerprint from its published working

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --offline

    Reads only the files in snapshots/epoch-<n>/ and rebuilds the merkle root. If it does not match the root posted on chain, we published a number we cannot justify.

    Re-derive it from chain instead of trusting the directory

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --recheck-chain

    Replays every caller’s transfer history from a node and recomputes every hold. This is the check that does not take our word for anything except the callout list.

    Confirm your own callout exists, without us

    curl -H "x-api-key: <pump.fun's public key>" \
  https://api.coin-communities.xyz/api/v1/users/by-wallet/<address>/callouts

    The same request this page makes from your browser. It is pump.fun’s record, not ours.

    risks

    What this is not

    The parts we would rather you read here than discover later. Open any of them.

    This is a memecoin. It can go to zero, and most do. What is described here decides how creator fees are split among holders who call it out. It is not a return, it is not a yield, and nothing on this page is a projection of what the coin will be worth. Nothing here is financial advice.

    The caller list is attested, not trustless

    Every number here except one can be re-derived by a stranger from public chain history. The exception is who called out — that exists only in pump.fun’s database. We read it and publish exactly what we read. You can check any individual callout against pump.fun directly, but you cannot re-derive the full list without us. We say attested rather than trustless because the second word would be false.

    Flags are pump.fun’s, and they are retroactive

    Callouts marked spam or harmful, or deleted, do not count. Those flags belong to pump.fun and can change after the fact. They are published in each day’s working so the rule stays checkable.

    The 24-hour window deters, it does not prevent

    Between a day’s results being posted and the money going out there are 24 hours in which anyone can spot a bad result — and nobody can stop it. There is no pause, no veto and no dispute button.

    If pump.fun removes callouts

    Everything earned up to that point stays claimable. Results already posted are unaffected — the program has no notion of callouts and cannot be switched off. New days would simply stop being created.
