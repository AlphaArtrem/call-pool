    What counts as selling

    Any decrease is a sale, however small. Sending tokens to another wallet you own counts. There is no netting, no summing across accounts, and no exemption for housekeeping. A decrease locks the wallet out of earning for the next 7 epochs, and buying back does not shorten it.

    Only the associated token account counts. Tokens parked in any other token account earn nothing — the daily settlement and the on-chain check both read the wallet’s associated account and nothing else.

    A wallet that has sold out entirely cannot be paid. The claim instruction checks that the recipient still holds the coin. Because rewards are sent daily rather than monthly, the exposure is one epoch — but it is real.

    Where the fees go

    Every trade on pump.fun pays a creator fee. Ours is split two ways, and the split was fixed before the coin ever traded:

    90% → the caller pool. Distributed every epoch to holders who called the coin out and held their position through it. Live balance · every past epoch

    10% → infrastructure. It also pays the gas that airdrops every reward daily, so you never claim anything. The daily settlement job reads the callout feed and replays each caller’s transfer history from chain. That needs a paid RPC, a host that isn’t someone’s laptop, and a domain. This line pays for those.

    It is not a founder’s fee, and here is how you can tell. It only pays anything if the coin trades. At $1M of weekly volume after graduation the whole creator fee is about $500 — roughly $450 to callers and $50 to infrastructure. If this coin does nothing, that line earns nothing and the job gets paid for out of pocket anyway, because the alternative is the settlement quietly stopping and nobody getting paid at all.

    Neither number can be changed. Not by us. pump.fun’s fee-sharing instruction sets the split and revokes its own authority in the same transaction — in their documentation, “this instruction can only effectively be used once.” We wrote 90/10 once, and that was the only chance anyone had to write anything.

    The transaction that set it

    What we do still control, and what we don’t. We hold the key that posts each epoch’s results, and what that key can and cannot do is spelled out in what this is not. We do not hold any authority over this split. pump.fun retains the same platform-level powers over this coin that they retain over every coin they host, including the ability to reassign a coin’s fee recipient — that is real, we cannot prevent it, and we would rather you read it here than discover it later.

    The rule

    eligible(w, d)  =  called_out(w, d)
                   AND  hold(w, d) >= 
                   AND  NOT locked(w, d)

hold(w, d)      =  the MINIMUM balance of w's associated token
                   account at any point during UTC day d

locked(w, d)    =  w's balance decreased during any of the 7
                   whole epochs before d

share(w, d)     =  pool(d) × hold(w, d) / Σ hold(v, d)
    The floor, as this page has it of the total supply, fixed on chain and immutable. It is a token count. There is no dollar figure and no price anywhere in this system.

    Verify it yourself

    These are the commands that would catch us if any number on this page were wrong. They need no key and no permission from us.

    The pool balance, straight from an RPC

    solana balance <pool address> --url devnet

    Reproduce an epoch’s root from its published directory

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --offline

    Reads only the files in snapshots/epoch-<n>/ and rebuilds the merkle root. If it does not match the root posted on chain, we published a number we cannot justify.

    Re-derive it from chain instead of trusting the directory

    node scripts/verify-epoch.mjs --epoch <n> --recheck-chain

    Replays every caller’s transfer history from an RPC and recomputes every hold. This is the check that does not take our word for anything except the callout list.

    Confirm your own callout exists, without us

    curl -H "x-api-key: <pump.fun's public key>" \
  https://api.coin-communities.xyz/api/v1/users/by-wallet/<address>/callouts

    The same request this page makes from your browser. It is pump.fun’s record, not ours.

    What this is not

    The snapshot key, stated precisely

    A stolen snapshot key takes the pool’s current balance and every future creator fee, permanently, for roughly the cost of the minimum holding. It cannot touch your tokens and it cannot change any parameter. There is no key rotation, so the only remedy would be to abandon the coin.

    The key is a 2-of-3 multisig held across independent hosts, which makes theft harder and loss survivable. It does not change the bound above.

    The caller list is attested, not trustless

    Every number here except one can be re-derived by a stranger from public chain history. The exception is who called out: that exists only in pump.fun’s database, we read it, and we publish exactly what we read. You can check any individual callout against pump.fun directly — the command is above — but you cannot re-derive the full list without us. We say attested rather than trustless because the second word would be false.

    The challenge window does not stop anything

    Between a root being posted and the payout going out there are 24 hours in which anyone can discover a bad root, and nobody can stop it. There is no pause, no veto and no dispute instruction. The window deters; it does not prevent.

    Flags are pump.fun’s, and they are retroactive

    Callouts marked spam or harmful, or deleted, do not count. Those flags belong to pump.fun, can change after the fact, and are published in each epoch’s snapshot so the rule stays checkable.

    If pump.fun removes callouts

    Everything earned up to that point stays claimable. Roots already posted are unaffected — the program has no notion of callouts and cannot be switched off. Epochs would simply stop being created.

    Risk

    This is a memecoin. It can go to zero, and most do. The mechanic described here decides how creator fees are split among holders who call it out; it is not a return, it is not a yield, and nothing on this page is a projection of what the coin will be worth. Nothing here is financial advice.
